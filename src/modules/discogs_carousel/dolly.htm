R.I.P. Dolly Parton
Dolly Parton has always had that down-to-earth, "every-lady" personality. Her lyrics are connected to reality, beautiful and in no way filled with pretentiousness.

Her sound is definitely Country Pop, but sometimes a song will sound more like Country Folk and be a bit more traditional. She's a true artist and not just a Pop star.

I also had no idea she wrote the original "I Will Always Love You". Her voice is sweet and soft and fits the music well. The album had a nice, well-rounded, complete vibe to it. It was alright.

Favorites:
Jolene

Admittedly this is my first experience with Dolly outside of just hearing the odd one-off song and yeah, it’s right after she died but whatever. She has an incredible voice, but everyone knows that. It’s (for me) between her and Emmylou Harris as the premiere female country voice. The songwriting is great, some classic tunes and fantastic mixes of country sounds and pop structures. It doesn’t feel ridiculously schmaltzy or super poppy, it feels rooted in country.
The two big ones on this are obviously great but I also loved When Someone Wants to Leave, Early Morning Breeze, and Randy are also great.
I wish I had gotten to this sooner, this is so good.

See ya soon, Dolly
Dolly Parton is a very important figure in the South, in music as a whole, and in the world, and I'm absolutely heartbroken to hear of her passing. Ms. Parton was a country Icon who revolutionized the genre, along with pioneering the sound of Nashville and countrypolitan. Her heart was always in the music, always in the performances, always with the ones who listened to her. America is a country full of amazing talent, but she closed a chapter of American music that will forever be remembered in history.

She modernized the country sound from the long-gone era of trail songs and ballads from the Old West; of course, she lived it. A Tennessee girl without a doubt, she loved where she was from and made sure she never left her folks behind. No one else has done it similar to her.

What makes Dolly an important person to me is that she was an ally to the queer folks of the South and 'round the world. Growing up as a gay boy in Texas, you had to listen to people who never liked us, and the musicians they listened to never supported us either; they never fought or voted to help us. Dolly was different. She was a country girl through and through, but she knew where humanity was; the care in her heart helped us, even if it was through her music. Being queer in the South, especially Texas, is turbulent, but I'm so glad that I knew Dolly was on my side even though she probably never knew I existed; that's who she is to me.

I have a distinct memory of when I was a young teen, being around a group of people I knew for certain would not like me, the ones who would disown their children for being queer types. They were listening to country while drinking on the back porch, many different artists, but then Dolly came on. At that moment, I knew that the only two people who accepted queerness was me and her.

“Why can’t they be as miserable as us heterosexuals in their marriages?” - Dolly Parton, 2014

RIP Dolly
HIGH 9

Dolly Parton is someone I don't know too much about, I never even listened to her music, yet hearing about her death hurt me a surprising amount. I've always been a huge country fan (REAL country, not bro-country or whatever tf), I find that it consistently has amazing melodies and always feels very sentimental/nostalgic in its delivery and vibe. This album is no exception, and considering the news, it hit way harder. I loved the vocals, the melodies, the instrumentation, the writing mixed with her tone, everything about this really. This does lose the slightest amount of points in having a couple of tracks that were just barely but noticeably weaker than the rest for me, but I thought this was a very consistent album.

Fav tracks: I Will Always Love You, Jolene, When Someone Wants to Leave
